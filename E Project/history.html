Our History

The Story of the Mountains

Mountains are more than beautiful landscapes. They carry stories of nature, people, exploration and adventure.

A Journey Through Time

A Journey Through Time

Mountains have played an important role throughout history. Early mountain communities built their lives around these giant landscapes, using them for water, farming and shelter.

Later, travelers and explorers crossed difficult mountain routes to discover new lands, trade goods and connect distant places. Over time, these same routes turned into paths for tourism, and today mountains welcome people from all over the world for modern adventure.

Legends of the Land

Mountains That Made History

Himalayas

Known for some of the highest peaks on Earth, including Mount Everest.

Karakoram

Famous for dramatic peaks and challenging climbing routes, including K2.

Alps

A major destination for European mountain tourism and adventure travel.

Rockies

Known for beautiful landscapes, wildlife and outdoor activities.

Hunza Valley

An ancient princely state along the Silk Road, known for its dramatic peaks and centuries of trade history.

Skardu and Deosai

The historic gateway for expeditions into the Karakoram, staging ground for attempts on K2.

Fairy Meadows

Gateway to Nanga Parbat, the Killer Mountain, site of mountaineering's most dramatic expeditions.

Swat Valley

Once the heart of ancient Gandhara civilization, later a flourishing center of Buddhist art and trade.

Naran Kaghan

A valley rich in local legend, centered around the glacial Saif ul Malook Lake and its folklore.

Chitral and Kalash

Home to the Kalash people, one of the last communities to preserve a pre-Islamic way of life.

Our Heroes

Legends Who Made History

Every peak has a first footstep. These climbers pushed beyond what was thought possible and changed mountaineering forever.

1786
Balmat & Paccard

Made the first ascent of Mont Blanc, an achievement widely regarded as the birth of modern mountaineering.

1953
Hillary & Norgay

Became the first climbers confirmed to reach the summit of Mount Everest, the highest point on Earth.

1954
Compagnoni & Lacedelli

Completed the first successful summit of K2, one of the most dangerous peaks in the world.

1975
Junko Tabei

Became the first woman to reach the summit of Mount Everest, inspiring generations of climbers worldwide.

1978
Reinhold Messner

First to summit Everest without supplemental oxygen, later becoming the first to climb all fourteen eight thousand meter peaks.

2019
Nirmal Purja

Climbed all fourteen eight thousand meter peaks in under seven months, shattering the previous record of nearly eight years.

Culture & Community

Life Among the Mountains

Mountain communities have learned to live in harmony with their surroundings for generations. Traditional lifestyles are shaped by the land, the weather and the seasons.

Local culture, small mountain villages and farming on steep slopes all reflect a deep connection to nature. Today, many of these communities also welcome tourism, sharing their way of life with travelers from around the world.
